# QuietOps narration — George (bm_george)

The shutters are down. The spreadsheets are still open. Stock, deliveries, invoices: one more evening spent looking for the small thing that could go wrong tomorrow.

This fictional shop has sixty products, twelve suppliers, and ninety days of sales. Twelve review candidates surface.

Original files stay intact. Confirmed mappings and recorded hashes keep every flag traceable.

Stock cover compares recent sales with supplier lead time. The lowest cover comes first, with estimates clearly marked.

A short inbox replaces the hunt across rows.

Oat milk has four units left. A hundred and seventy-four sales over ninety days gives just over two days of cover.

The supplier needs five days. But missing sales count as zero, and incoming orders still need checking. The assumptions stay visible.

Now Ministral reads the evidence locally. It explains the flag and proposes a next step. Source and number checks help, but the wording still needs human judgement.

The proposed next step becomes a purchase review draft, ready for an owner to inspect.

Review the wording, verify the sources, and edit where needed. The model helps write the request; it does not make the decision.

Approval makes the draft available for export. It sends no email, places no order, and changes no financial record.

The audit trail records the check, model explanation, approval, and export. Each step stays accountable.

In this synthetic run, thousands of rows become twelve candidates and one approved draft. Actual owner time savings still need a field study. Catch the small things. Keep your evening.
